## What I understood

kernel development and optimization using LLMs is a important thing now
this paper presents an agent for kernel optimization - the novelty is that its question driven (first by a humans questions and then a question bank)
minimal harness + verifiable goal + question bank 
takes a working CUDA kernel, a set of scenarios, and a goal, and returns a faster kernel whose outputs match the seed on every scenario
a predicate such as “at least parity with the seed on every scenario” removes the most common failure of autonomous agents, declaring completion early

## Gaps

i think its a very new approach, and deeper kernels are not working with it yet. the next steps are to apply the same to more complex, deeper kernels

what i want to read more about though is how a question driven approach can be applied to other situations. sometimes when we give llms a long list of instructions, it may not follow every line to the T. but instead if we give it questions to answer and evaluate itself on, it might find vulnerabilities and gaps better? for example-make sure xyz happens vs has xyz happened
or not just for llms writing code. this could be applied to a lot of places
